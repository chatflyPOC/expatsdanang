## Priority logic

Effort follows **commercial value × competitive weakness**, not search volume. We have no volume data (no DataForSEO), so ordering is driven by observed SERP strength and proximity to revenue — both of which we *can* assess.

That produces a counter-intuitive ranking: our most-trafficked topic (cost of living) is the **lowest** priority, because Numbeo, Expatistan and Nomads.com own it structurally. Our least glamorous topic (visa paperwork) is the **highest**, because one of the top-ranking competitors is a Facebook page.

## Cadence

**One substantial page per week**, sustained. Better than bursts — freshness signals reward consistency, and quarterly YMYL reviews need slack in the schedule.

## Writing standards

Every page ships with:

- **A direct answer in the first 40–60 words.** Currently our openings are scene-setting ("Da Nang is one of the best-value coastal cities in Asia…") and bury the number. Lead with the number.
- **Self-contained passages of 130–170 words** so an LLM can quote a block without needing surrounding context.
- **Real, dated, attributed figures.** Specificity is what makes content citable.
- **A named author with genuine credentials** — "3 years running motorbike logistics in Da Nang", not "Expats Da Nang Team".
- **Populated sources.** An empty "Sources & references" heading is worse than none.
- **Minimum 1,500 words for pillars, 800 for service pages.** No current guide meets the pillar floor (range: 649–1,108).
- **One service-page link**, matching the guide's `service` field.

## A caution on figures

The audit surfaced draft rewrites containing plausible-looking prices that **did not appear anywhere on the source pages**. Verified examples (500,000 VND/day overstay fine, `evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn`) were real; invented ones ($900–1,300/month, $420 apartments) were not.

Every number published must trace to your own booking data or a cited external source. Publishing invented prices on visa and housing pages is both a trust and a liability problem — these are YMYL topics where readers act on the figures.
