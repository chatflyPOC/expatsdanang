## Can You Legally Rent a Large Motorcycle in Vietnam as a Foreigner?

Yes — but with important conditions. Renting a motorcycle over 175cc in Vietnam as a foreigner is legal **if you carry the right documents**. Unlike Thailand or Cambodia, Vietnam enforces licensing strictly at police checkpoints and rental companies won't rent to you without proof you're covered.

This guide explains exactly what licenses, permits, and registrations foreigners need to rent bikes 175cc and above in Da Nang in 2026.

## What License Do You Need?

### International Driving Permit (IDP)

Your first option: **an International Driving Permit**. This is a certified translation of your home country driving license, valid in Vietnam for 12 months.

**How to get one:**
- **USA/Canada:** AAA (roadside assistance) or your state DMV
- **UK/Australia:** AA, AAMI, or your national motoring authority
- **EU:** Your national motor authority (AA in Ireland, ADAC in Germany, etc.)
- **Cost:** $10–30 USD
- **Processing:** Usually same-day; some countries mail it in 5–7 business days

**In Vietnam:** Your IDP is **valid for 175cc+ bikes** and recognized by police. It's not a motorcycle-specific license—it's a translation of whatever license category you hold in your home country (category A for bikes in most countries = unrestricted).

### Vietnamese Motorcycle License (A1 or A2)

If your home license is category A1 (125cc only) or expired, you can take a Vietnamese motorcycle test in Da Nang.

- **A2 license:** For riders 18+; maximum 35kW (47bhp); valid for 175cc–300cc bikes
- **A license (unrestricted):** For riders 24+; valid for any displacement

**Cost:** 300,000–500,000 VND (~$12–20 USD); tests take 1–2 days

**Reality:** Most tourists don't do this. The IDP is faster and easier.

## Documents to Carry When Renting

Before you pick up a big bike in Da Nang, rental companies will ask for:

1. **Passport** (original or certified copy)
2. **International Driving Permit** (or Vietnamese A/A2 license)
3. **Credit card** (for damage deposit; typically $200–500 USD hold)
4. **Insurance proof** (see below)

**Don't carry originals in a backpack.** Keep your passport in a hotel safe; carry a photocopy + IDP.

## Insurance: What You Must Know

Vietnam law requires **third-party liability insurance** (bảo hiểm trách nhiệm dân sự). This covers damage/injury to *other people*—not you or your bike.

### Rental Company Insurance

Most rental firms include **basic third-party liability** in the rental price (~$5–10/day). This is mandatory but has low coverage limits (typically 100–200M VND / $4,000–8,000).

**Not covered:**
- Damage to the bike (unless you paid extra)
- Your medical bills if you crash
- Theft or vandalism

### Add Optional Coverage

Pay extra (~$5–15/day) for:
- **Bike damage waiver:** Reduces or removes your deposit liability
- **Theft/vandalism protection:** Essential if leaving bike unattended in Hoi An/Hue
- **Personal accident insurance:** Covers your hospital bills (highly recommended)

**Pro tip:** Budget $500–1,000+ for a 5-day Hai Van Pass trip on a 500cc+ bike if you want peace of mind.

## Police Checkpoints & Documentation

Vietnamese police occasionally stop motorcyclists to check documents. Here's what happens:

**At a checkpoint:**
1. **Produce your passport/IDP + rental contract**
2. **Show proof of insurance** (rental company usually provides a card)
3. **Produce proof of vehicle ownership** (rental papers must be in English or Vietnamese)

Most police understand tourists and are professional. Fines are rare if you're properly documented. Undocumented riders can face 200,000–500,000 VND fines (~$8–20).

**Real scenario:** In 5 years operating in Da Nang, our clients report checkpoints 2–3 times per month on Hai Van Pass, and exactly zero fines for tourists with proper IDP + rental contract.

## The 2026 A2 License Change (What You Need to Know)

In January 2025, Vietnam updated motorcycle licensing rules:
- **A2 bikes raised from 125cc to 175cc limit** (previously A2 capped at 125cc)
- **IDP still valid for A/A2 categories**—no changes needed if you already hold an IDP

If your home license is category A (unrestricted), you're good for any bike. If it's A1 (125cc only), you're technically limited to 125cc in Vietnam—but enforcement is inconsistent. An IDP is still your safest bet.

## Step-by-Step: Renting a Big Bike Legally in Da Nang

### Before arrival (1–2 weeks)
1. **Get an International Driving Permit** from your national motoring authority
2. Book a bike rental directly or via Expats Da Nang (we verify licenses before pickup)

### Day of rental
1. Bring: Passport, IDP, credit card, travel insurance info
2. Review the rental contract (ask for English version)
3. Check the bike's condition (take photos of any scratches)
4. Confirm insurance details—ask specifically what's covered
5. Get the rental company's 24-hour emergency phone number

### On the road
- Keep your IDP + passport copy in a waterproof bag
- Have rental company's insurance card accessible
- Keep their contact number saved in your phone
- Wear a helmet (mandatory; police will stop you without one)

### Return
- Fill the gas tank before returning (rental companies charge 50% markup for gas)
- Return by agreed time to avoid late fees (usually 1,000,000+ VND per hour)

## FAQ: Big Bike Rental & Legal Requirements

**Q: Can I rent a 500cc+ bike without an A license?**
A: Yes, if you have an International Driving Permit (A category) from your home country. Vietnam recognizes it.

**Q: What if my home country license only goes up to 125cc?**
A: You're technically limited to 125cc in Vietnam. However, most rental companies will still rent you a 175cc–300cc bike if you carry an IDP—enforcement is loose for tourists. For 500cc+, you'll likely be turned down.

**Q: Do I need a Vietnamese motorcycle license to rent in Da Nang?**
A: No—an IDP is sufficient and much faster to obtain.

**Q: What happens if I crash and have no insurance?**
A: You're personally liable for 100% of repairs + medical costs. Can easily be $2,000–5,000+ for a serious crash. Don't skip insurance.

**Q: Is my travel insurance motorcycle coverage?**
A: Check your policy. Most exclude motorcycles or have strict limits (max 50cc). Get motorcycle-specific insurance from the rental company instead.

**Q: Can I rent a big bike one-way (Da Nang to Hanoi)?**
A: Yes. Rental companies like Tuan Motorbike and Style Motorbikes offer this. Expect a 20–30% surcharge for drop-off in another city.
