# Unfurnished Apartment Rental: What's Included & What You Need to Buy

**Published:** September 2026 | **Read time:** 5 minutes

Unfurnished apartments in Da Nang are 20–30% cheaper than furnished. This guide explains what's typically included, what you need to buy, and hidden costs.

## Unfurnished: What's TYPICALLY Included

### Fixed to the Building (NOT taking with you on move-out)

- ✅ Air conditioning unit (window or split unit)
- ✅ Ceiling fans
- ✅ Lighting fixtures (overhead lights)
- ✅ Toilet + bidet (Vietnamese style)
- ✅ Sink + faucets
- ✅ Stove/cooktop (built-in, if kitchen present)
- ✅ Curtain rods (but not curtains)

### Usually NOT Included (You provide)

- ❌ Beds, mattresses
- ❌ Sofas, armchairs
- ❌ Kitchen table + chairs
- ❌ Nightstands, dressers
- ❌ Cooking utensils (pots, pans, dishes)
- ❌ Curtains (just rods)
- ❌ Lamps, side lights
- ❌ Refrigerator
- ❌ Washing machine
- ❌ Microwave
- ❌ Decorations, wall art

## Hidden Costs of Unfurnished Living

| Cost | Monthly | Annually | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furniture amortized (one-time $1000 / 12 months) | $83 | $1,000 | Spread upfront cost |
| Utilities (water, electricity) | $30 | $360 | Unfurnished = no AC included; higher usage |
| Internet | $3 | $36 | Usually separate |
| Maintenance (light bulbs, repairs) | $10 | $120 | Your responsibility |
| **Total added cost** | **$126** | **$1,516** | - |

**Comparison:**
- Furnished: $300/month (all-in)
- Unfurnished: $200 + $126 = $326/month (actual cost including furniture + utilities)

**Reality check:** Unfurnished only saves ~$80/month if you account for utilities + furniture amortization.

## The Appliance Question: What to Buy vs Avoid

### Should You Buy a Washing Machine?

**Arguments for:** $150–250 one-time; saves 1 hour/week laundry shopping

**Arguments against:**
- You're moving eventually; can't take it
- Plumbing complications (installation + removal)
- Repair costs ($50+ if breaks)

**Solution:** Use laundry service instead
- Hand washing (free; time cost)
- Laundry shop (2kg = $2–3; 1–2 hours turnaround)
- Maid service (includes washing; $10–15/week)

**Verdict:** Skip washing machine; use laundry service

### Should You Buy a Microwave?

**Arguments for:** $30–50; convenience (reheat food instantly)

**Arguments against:**
- Kitchen may not have electrical outlet near counter
- Limited use (Vietnamese cooking doesn't use microwave much)

**Solution:** Buy basic (yes)
- Cost: $30 (used; from Facebook)
- Utility: ~3x/week
- Durability: Lasts 3+ years

**Verdict:** Buy one (low cost; useful)

### Should You Buy a Refrigerator?

**Arguments for:** Essential for food storage

**Arguments against:**
- Expensive ($200–400 new)
- Big/heavy (hard to move)
- Can't take when leaving

**Solution:**
- Landlord often provides used fridge ($0 cost)
- Ask at signing: "Does apartment have fridge?"
- Many unfurnished come with used fridge (included)

**Verdict:** Included in most places; if not, ask landlord to provide

## Customs & Import Duties

**If bringing furniture from your home country:**

### Duty-Free Threshold
- Personal goods: $0 duty if "for personal use" + used condition
- New furniture: 10–20% import duty

**Reality:** Customs rarely charges expats on household goods. Flying in furniture is impractical anyway (shipping costs exceed furniture value).

### Shipping to Da Nang
- Sea freight from US: $2,000–5,000 (container)
- Fewer from UK/Australia: $3,000–7,000
- Domestic (from Hanoi/HCMC): $200–400

**Verdict:** Fly with checked luggage (suitcase) only. Buy locally; it's cheaper and easier.

## Damage Deposits & Move-Out Condition

### Landlord Inspection (Move-in)

**Before moving in:**
- Take photos of all walls, floors, ceiling (document current state)
- Check appliances (working/broken)
- List any damage in writing with landlord (photo-date it)

**Why:** Prevents landlord charging you for pre-existing damage on move-out.

### Move-Out Condition Expectations

**Normal wear:**
- Scuff marks on walls
- Faded paint (3+ years)
- Minor carpet wear

**Not normal (you pay to repair):**
- Holes in walls
- Stains/water damage (furniture caused)
- Broken appliances (you broke them)

**Deposit return:** Typically 7–14 days if "normal" condition

## Furniture FAQ

**Q: Can I negotiate furniture as part of rent?**
A: Yes. If landlord says unfurnished but has leftover furniture, ask: "Can you leave the sofa for -$30/month rent discount?"

**Q: Do I need a sofa?**
A: No; single chair + bed works. Sofa is comfort, not necessity.

**Q: Where do I find used furniture?**
A: Facebook groups ("Da Nang Expats Buy Sell"); Airbnb hosts' friends (word-of-mouth); Marketplace.

**Q: Can I paint the walls?**
A: Ask landlord first. Most allow accent walls; require restore-to-white on move-out.

**Q: Furnish it myself or hire movers?**
A: Hire locals ($5–10/item for delivery + assembly). DIY if handy.
