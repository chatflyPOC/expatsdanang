# Apartment Rental Scams: How to Avoid Common Fraud in Da Nang

**Published:** September 2026 | **Read time:** 5 minutes

Expats lose $500–5,000 annually to rental scams. This guide covers the 8 most common scams and how to avoid them.

## Scam #1: Fake Landlord (Craigslist/Facebook Impersonation)

### How it works:

1. You find apartment listing (beautiful furnished 1BR, $150/month)
2. Contact "landlord" via email/WhatsApp
3. "Landlord" sends photos; asks for deposit via Western Union/bank transfer
4. You send $300–500 deposit
5. Later, you show up to apartment; real landlord has no record of you; apartment is already rented to someone else

### Red flags:

- [ ] Landlord won't video call (only messages)
- [ ] Rent is 50% cheaper than neighborhood average (too good to be true)
- [ ] Won't let you visit apartment before payment
- [ ] Asks for Western Union/cryptocurrency (untraceable)
- [ ] Photos look professional (stolen from Airbnb/other listings)

### How to avoid:

1. **Always visit in person** before paying anything
2. **Verify ownership:** Ask landlord for ID + property registration doc
3. **Meet at property:** If landlord can't meet at the apartment, it's a scam
4. **Never prepay via untraceable method:** Use bank transfer (has paper trail)
5. **Reverse image search:** Google Images → upload apartment photo → check if it's from other listings

## Scam #2: Deposit Theft (Landlord Keeps Security Deposit)

### How it works:

1. You sign lease; pay $300 deposit + 2 months rent
2. 6 months later, you move out; apartment is clean
3. Landlord inspects; finds minor issues ("scratches on wall," "faded paint")
4. Claims damage costs $200; refunds you only $100 instead of full $300

### Red flags:

- [ ] Landlord won't write down damage policy in lease
- [ ] Vague about "normal wear" vs damage
- [ ] No inspection walk-through at move-out (you can't defend yourself)
- [ ] Landlord doesn't respond to move-out email
- [ ] Repair costs seem inflated ($100 for wall touch-up)

### How to avoid:

1. **Document move-in condition:** Take 20+ photos (walls, ceiling, floors, appliances)
2. **Email landlord:** "Attached are move-in photos. Agree this is current condition?" (get written confirmation)
3. **Lease clause:** "Deposit refunded within 7 days if apartment in normal condition"
4. **Move-out inspection:** Ask landlord to inspect WITH you (document agreed condition)
5. **Bank transfer deposit:** Traceable; easier to dispute than cash

## Scam #3: Inflated Utility Bills (Landlord Overcharges)

### How it works:

1. Landlord charges flat $80/month for utilities (instead of tiered 5,000–10,000 VND per kWh)
2. Your actual usage: 100 kWh/month = 50,000 VND (~$2.50)
3. Landlord's overcharge: You pay $80 vs actual $3 = $77 profit per month
4. Over 12 months: Landlord profits $924 from utilities alone

### Red flags:

- [ ] Landlord refuses to show utility meter or bills
- [ ] "Utilities included; flat rate, don't worry about it"
- [ ] No breakdown of electricity vs water costs
- [ ] Won't explain how they calculated flat rate

### How to avoid:

1. **Request itemized breakdown:** "Show me electricity bill + water bill"
2. **Meter access:** "I want to photograph meter monthly to verify usage"
3. **Negotiate separately:** "I'll pay electricity direct to utility company; just charge me rent + water"
4. **Document meter readings:** Monthly photos; email to yourself (proof if dispute)

## Scam #4: Double Rental (Landlord Rents Same Apartment Twice)

### How it works:

1. Landlord owns apartment; rents to You (contract signed)
2. Landlord rents SAME apartment to Another Expat (also signed; unknown to you)
3. You move in; Another Expat also shows up with keys
4. Chaos; police called; both are stuck; deposit is gone

### Red flags:

- [ ] Landlord can't provide property registration (legal proof)
- [ ] Won't let you verify ownership at local ward office
- [ ] Too many "interested renters" for same apartment (overselling)
- [ ] Landlord is agent, not owner (middleman; less accountability)

### How to avoid:

1. **Verify owner:** Ask for official property registration document (Giấy Chứng Nhận)
2. **Meet the owner:** Never rent from agent alone; speak to property owner
3. **Check with landlord's bank:** "Do you have mortgage on this property?" (legitimate owner can answer)
4. **Ward office verification:** Visit local administration office; ask "Is [address] owned by [landlord name]?" (free check)

## Scam #5: Hidden Contract Terms (Small Print Trap)

### How it works:

1. Lease looks normal; $300/month, 6-month term
2. Small print (Vietnamese only, hard to read) includes:
   - $50/month "maintenance fee" (not disclosed initially)
   - 10,000 VND per day late fee (compounding)
   - Early exit = forfeit 3 months rent (not 1 month)
3. You sign without reading fine print
4. Later, surprise charges or locked-in commitment

### Red flags:

- [ ] Lease is Vietnamese-only (you can't read it fully)
- [ ] Landlord rushes signing ("Need your signature today")
- [ ] Won't explain specific clauses when asked
- [ ] "Standard contract; don't worry about details"

### How to avoid:

1. **Get English translation:** Before signing, translate lease to English (hire translator; $20–30)
2. **Read every clause:** Especially rent amount, term, deposit, late fees, early exit penalty
3. **Negotiate changes:** If terms are unfair, ask landlord to modify before signing
4. **Take photos of signed copy:** Email to yourself (proof you signed it)

## Scam #6: Fake Lease (Landlord Backs Out After You Move In)

### How it works:

1. You sign lease (photocopy, informal)
2. Landlord says "I need to renovate; you have to leave in 2 weeks"
3. Lease wasn't registered with authorities (not official)
4. You have no legal recourse; lose deposit + move-out costs

### Red flags:

- [ ] Lease is informal (handwritten, not official form)
- [ ] No landlord company letterhead or official stamp
- [ ] Landlord says "We don't need to register it officially; just between us"
- [ ] No copy for you; landlord keeps only version

### How to avoid:

1. **Official lease:** Use standard Vietnamese rental contract (landlord should provide)
2. **Registration:** Lease should be registered with local ward office (cost: 50,000 VND)
3. **Keep copy:** Get two signed copies; you keep one always
4. **Verify with ward office:** "Is this lease registered under my name?" (free verification)

## Scam #7: "Damage Claims" Post-Move-In

### How it works:

1. You move into apartment; everything looks clean
2. Week 2: Landlord claims "water damage in ceiling from previous tenant" (from last year)
3. Landlord deducts $500 from your deposit; claims you're responsible
4. You protest; landlord threatens to involve police

### Red flags:

- [ ] Landlord discovers "damage" 2+ weeks after you move in
- [ ] Damage looks old (water stains, faded paint)
- [ ] Landlord didn't mention damage at move-in
- [ ] No photos from move-in to compare

### How to avoid:

1. **Move-in walk-through:** With photos + landlord signature (both agree on current condition)
2. **Time-stamp photos:** Date/time stamp proves when photos were taken
3. **Email confirmation:** "I confirm apartment is in [good/described] condition" from landlord
4. **Insurance:** Take rental insurance ($3–5/month) that covers landlord disputes

## Scam #8: Fake Agency (Middleman Scam)

### How it works:

1. "Agency" on Facebook offers furnished apartments
2. You pay "booking fee" ($50) + first month ($300)
3. Agency hands you keys
4. Week 1: Real landlord shows up; never authorized rental
5. You're evicted; "agency" has disappeared; $350 gone

### Red flags:

- [ ] Agent isn't the property owner
- [ ] Asks for "booking fee" upfront (not standard)
- [ ] Won't introduce you to owner/landlord
- [ ] No official business registration
- [ ] Communications only via WhatsApp (no official address/phone)

### How to avoid:

1. **Always meet owner:** Never rent through middleman/agent alone
2. **No booking fees:** Legitimate landlords don't charge "booking" fees (only deposit at signing)
3. **Verify business:** Ask for business registration number; check online
4. **Bank transfer only:** Pay only via bank; traceable if dispute

## Scam Prevention Checklist

Before signing lease:

- [ ] Visit apartment in person; verify it exists (not fake photos)
- [ ] Meet property owner (not agent alone)
- [ ] Verify ownership (registration doc or ward office check)
- [ ] Read lease in English (translated if needed)
- [ ] Get written damage policy + move-in inspection photos
- [ ] Negotiate meter access (utilities tracking)
- [ ] Register lease officially (ward office; 50k VND)
- [ ] Keep copies of everything (lease, ID, payment receipts)

---

## If You're Scammed: Recovery Steps

1. **Document everything:** Gather emails, WhatsApp messages, photos, receipts, lease copy
2. **Report to police:** File complaint at local police station (Công an)
3. **Contact landlord legally:** Send formal letter (via email + registered mail) demanding refund + specific deadline
4. **Involve landlord's landlord:** If middleman, contact property owner directly (often unaware of scam)
5. **Expat community:** Post warning on "Da Nang Expats" Facebook (help others avoid same scam)
6. **Mediation:** Ask Da Nang Expats group to mediate (many scammers refund when publicly called out)
7. **Accept loss:** Most disputes resolve to partial refund (60–70% recovery); total recovery is rare

**Reality:** Vietnamese civil courts take 6–12 months; expats often give up. Prevention is better than recovery.

## FAQ

**Q: I lost $300 to a fake landlord. Can I get it back?**
A: Contact landlord via email with police report attached; threaten to post on Da Nang Expats Facebook (surprisingly effective). Expect 50–70% recovery.

**Q: Is renting through agency safer than direct landlord?**
A: No. Legitimate agencies help but add 10–20% fee. Direct landlord is usually cheaper + safer.

**Q: What's the most common scam?**
A: Deposit theft (landlord invents damage on move-out). Prevent with photos + written terms.

**Q: Should I buy rental insurance?**
A: Yes ($3–5/month); covers landlord disputes + liability.
