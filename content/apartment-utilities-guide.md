# Apartment Utilities Explained: Electricity, Water & Backup Power in Da Nang

**Published:** September 2026 | **Read time:** 5 minutes

Tropical living in Da Nang means high AC usage, frequent power cuts, and water concerns. This guide covers utility costs, billing, and backup solutions.

## Monthly Utility Costs at a Glance

| Utility | 1BR Apartment | Unfurnished (higher usage) | Furnished (AC set cool) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electricity** | $40–80 | $60–100 (AC 24/7) | $80–120 |
| **Water** | $3–5 | $5–8 | $5–8 |
| **Internet** | $5–15 | $5–15 | $5–15 |
| **Waste/maintenance** | $5–10 | $5–10 | $5–10 |
| **Total/month** | **$53–110** | **$75–143** | **$95–153** |

**Biggest variable:** Air conditioning (AC) usage. Running AC 24/7 in summer = $100+/month electricity alone.

## Electricity (VND per kWh)

### Pricing Structure (2026)

Vietnam uses tiered pricing: the more you use, the higher the per-kWh rate.

| Usage Tier | Rate (per kWh) | Monthly Cost Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0–50 kWh | 1,600 VND ($0.07) | $3.50 for 50 kWh |
| 51–100 kWh | 1,920 VND ($0.08) | $10 for 50 kWh in this tier |
| 101–150 kWh | 2,320 VND ($0.10) | $14.50 for 50 kWh in this tier |
| 150+ kWh | 2,920 VND ($0.125) | $29+ for 50 kWh in this tier |

**Example:**
- Summer month (heavy AC): 250 kWh usage
- Tier 1 (50 kWh): $3.50
- Tier 2 (50 kWh): $10
- Tier 3 (50 kWh): $14.50
- Tier 4 (100 kWh): $29
- **Total: ~$57 for 250 kWh**

### How to Reduce Electricity Costs

| Strategy | Savings | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| AC 20°C vs 16°C | $5–10/month | Low (adjust thermostat) |
| AC 8 hours/day vs 24 | $30–50/month | Medium (sleep in heat some nights) |
| Use fan + AC (fan alone at night) | $15–20/month | Medium (tolerate humidity) |
| Unplug devices when not using | $2–5/month | High effort, low savings |
| Laundry during off-peak (early morning) | Minimal | Low (not worth changing habits) |
| **Most effective: Reduce AC hours** | **$30–50/month** | **Medium effort** |

### Electricity Meter Reading

**Landlord's responsibility:** Most landlords pay/bill you for actual usage (documented on meter)

**What to expect:**
- Monthly meter reading (landlord photos it or you photograph it yourself)
- Bill = current reading minus previous month's reading
- You pay tiered rate based on your kWh

**Red flag:** If landlord won't show meter, charges flat $80/month "regardless of usage" = likely overcharging

## Water (VND per m³)

### Residential Water Rates (2026)

| Usage Tier | Rate per m³ | Monthly Cost Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 m³ | 5,550 VND ($0.24/m³) | $1 for 4 m³ |
| 4–8 m³ | 7,140 VND ($0.31/m³) | $3.50 for 4 m³ |
| 8+ m³ | 8,530 VND ($0.37/m³) | $3.70 per m³ above 8 |

**Typical usage:** 5–8 m³/month for 1 person (shower daily, laundry, cooking, AC cooling)

**Cost:** $2–5/month for most expats

### Water Quality Concerns

**Tap water safety:** Vietnamese tap water is not safe to drink directly

**Expat options:**
1. **Drink bottled water** ($0.30–0.50 per 1.5L bottle; $10–15/month)
2. **Water cooler + dispenser** (rent dispenser for $3/month; buy water bottles $3 each; 4 bottles/month = $15–18/month total)
3. **Install water filter system** ($50 one-time + $15–20/month filter replacements)

**Most popular:** Water cooler (convenience + low cost)

## Internet (The Remote Worker Essential)

### Providers & Speeds

| Provider | Speed Tier | Cost | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|
| FPT Telecom | 50 Mbps | $12/month | Excellent; rarely cuts out |
| Viettel | 30 Mbps | $8/month | Good; occasional midday slowdown |
| Mobifone | 10 Mbps | $5/month | Budget; inconsistent |
| **Recommendation for WFH** | **FPT 50 Mbps** | **$12–15/month** | **Best value** |

### Setup

**Who arranges:** Usually landlord (for furnished apartments) or you (for unfurnished)

**Installation:** Free; technician comes to apartment; takes 1 hour

**Contract:** Month-to-month (you can cancel anytime; no minimum)

**Backup:** Also get mobile hotspot (Viettel SIM card; $10 credit = 8GB; use as internet backup for video calls)

## Power Cuts & Backup Solutions

### How Often Do Power Cuts Happen?

| Time of Year | Frequency | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Dry season (Sep–Nov) | Very rare (<1/month) | 10–30 min |
| Monsoon (Jun–Aug) | Occasional (2–3/month) | 30–120 min |
| Hot season (Mar–May) | Moderate (1–2/month) | 15–45 min |

**Truth:** Power cuts are annoying but predictable. Landlords/locals are used to them.

### Backup Power Options

| Solution | Cost | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UPS (battery backup)** | $50–100 | Powers laptop + lights for 2 hours | Only covers small load |
| **Power bank** | $30–60 | Charges phone + laptop | Needs charging itself |
| **Portable generator** | $100–300 | Runs AC + lights + fans | Loud; needs fuel; expensive |
| **Do nothing** | $0 | No cost | Laptop dies if unsaved work |

**For remote workers:** Combine UPS (for work laptop/WiFi router) + power bank (phone), NOT generator (too loud for video calls during outage).

### What Remote Workers Should Prepare

- [ ] UPS battery backup ($50–80) for laptop + WiFi router
- [ ] Power bank ($30–50) for phone
- [ ] Offline work capability (download files; prepare work that doesn't need internet)
- [ ] Mobile hotspot backup (Viettel SIM + credits)

**Cost:** $100–150 one-time investment; protects your income.

## Generator Power vs Grid

**Landlord question:** Does apartment have backup generator?

**What it means:**
- Common building generators (older buildings, high-end apartments)
- Activate automatically during grid outages
- Slightly higher electricity cost (you pay for generator fuel + maintenance)
- But lights/fans/AC work during power cuts

**Cost impact:** Pays for itself if you value uninterrupted power (remote workers benefit; casual expats don't care).

## Utility Billing & Payment

### Who Pays?

| Scenario | Landlord Pays | Tenant Pays |
|---|---|---|
| Furnished apartment | Pays + bills you (included in rent or separate) | You | Not usually separate; check lease |
| Unfurnished apartment | Usually tenant | You | Landlord provides meter; you pay direct |

### How Billing Works

**Direct from utility company:**
- You set up account (landlord helps)
- Meter in apartment (you photograph monthly)
- Payment: Online (Ngân Hàng) or in-person at utility office

**Through landlord:**
- Landlord reads meter
- Bills you (cash or bank transfer)
- You pay landlord

**Tip:** Photograph the meter yourself each month (protects you from disputes).

## FAQ

**Q: Why is my electricity bill so high (100+/month)?**
A: Heavy AC usage. Summer 24/7 AC = $80–100. Use fan at night, or set AC to 18°C only for sleeping.

**Q: Is tap water drinkable?**
A: No. Use water cooler or bottled water for drinking/cooking. Tap is fine for showers.

**Q: What's the WiFi like for video calls?**
A: FPT 50 Mbps is reliable for video (recommend this). Viettel sometimes gets congested midday. Always have mobile hotspot backup.

**Q: Do I need a generator for backup power?**
A: No (too loud). UPS battery + mobile hotspot is sufficient for remote work.

**Q: How do I reduce AC costs?**
A: Set to 20°C (not 16°C). Run 8 hours/day (not 24). Use fan only at night. Saves $30–50/month.
